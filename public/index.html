            How scoring works

            Every car is worth 10 points. Answers are read against the whole car list, so the game knows that “918” is the 918 Spyder and that “Supra” could be two different cars. The server checks every answer, so the score you see is the score that counts.

            Cars

            Make
            2, whether you type it or the model gives it away: “918” means Porsche.
            Model
            5 for an answer that pins down the car. 3 for the right model with the trim or generation missing (M4 for an M4 Competition), or a name that fits more than one car (Supra: Mk4 or Mk5?). 1 for a designation that’s part of the name but doesn’t identify it (Spyder).

            3 for any year the car was built. Close years earn part of it, depending on the rules below.

            What counts

            • Type it the way you’d say it, all in one box: “Porsche 918 Spyder”, “918”, “R34 GT-R”, “GT3 RS”, “SVJ”. A shorter answer is just as good when it can only mean one car.
            • Nicknames, chassis codes and generation marks: Beemer, E46, FD, Mk4. Spacing and punctuation don’t matter.
            • A year typed in the same box (“1999 Skyline R34”) counts when the year box is empty.

            What doesn’t

            • Dropping the trim or generation. GT3 isn’t a GT3 RS and an M4 isn’t an M4 Competition, so those earn the 3-point partial.
            • Designations on their own. Spyder, RS or Competition could be dozens of cars, so they earn 1 point at most.
            • Naming more than one car. “Silvia Skyline 350Z” scores nothing for the model.
            • Partial words and shared letters. “Lam” isn’t Lamborghini.
            • Answers after the clock. In ranked games the server times every car.
